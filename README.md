# ⚡ IEC-CH Documentation

> 📘 A guide to understanding and applying international electrotechnical standards, so people and companies can work more safely, consistently, and confidently.

**IEC-CH Documentation** is an independent, community-maintained collection of original learning material about IEC standards: plain-language summaries, study notes, compliance checklists, flashcards, a glossary, and an index that links each standard to its official IEC Webstore page. It does **not** contain or reproduce the standards themselves.

> 🚧 **Status:** early stage. The structure, templates, and guidance are ready; original summaries and checklists are being added over time (see the [Roadmap](#-roadmap)).

## 🎯 What is this?

This repository helps people find, understand, and apply IEC standards in practice.

### 📦 What is included

| Content           | Description                                                    |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 📝 Summaries      | Short, plain-language explanations written in original words   |
| ✅ Checklists     | Practical compliance and audit checklists                      |
| 🃏 Study material | Flashcards, quizzes, and lesson ideas                          |
| 📖 Glossary       | Beginner-friendly definitions of common terms                  |
| 🔗 Index          | Links from each topic to the official IEC Webstore page        |
| 🧰 Templates      | Reusable formats for writing your own summaries and checklists |

### 🚫 What is not included

- Copies or excerpts of IEC standards or other IEC publications
- Any content that would let someone avoid purchasing the official documents

The goal is simple: make the knowledge inside international standards easier to learn from, easier to apply, and easier to share, while respecting the rights of the people who produce them.

## 🔬 Benefits in depth

### 🙋 For individuals

- 🧭 **Direction.** A clear reference tells you what "good" looks like, so you spend less time guessing.
- 📚 **Structured learning.** Standards are organized and precise, which makes them excellent backbones for self-study.
- 🪪 **Credibility.** Being able to cite the right standard in a discussion or report builds professional trust.
- 🔁 **Transferable skills.** Habits like documenting requirements, verifying evidence, and reviewing revisions help in any career.
- 🧩 **Better decisions.** Knowing the rules of your field helps you judge risk, cost, and trade-offs more confidently.
- 🌱 **Lifelong growth.** Standards evolve, so following updates keeps your skills current.

### 🎓 For students and teachers

- 🧱 **A bridge from theory to practice.** Standards show how classroom concepts are applied in real products and systems.
- 🗂️ **Ready-made structure for lessons.** Each topic can become a lesson, a quiz, a lab, or a project.
- 💼 **Employability.** Graduates who already know how standards work are easier to onboard.
- 🗣️ **Discussion material.** Real-world failures and compliance scenarios make engaging case studies.
- 🤝 **Collaboration.** Shared references let students in different schools or countries work on the same problems.

### 🏢 For companies and teams

- 🛡️ **Risk reduction.** Following recognized requirements lowers the chance of safety incidents, recalls, and disputes.
- 💸 **Cost control.** Catching problems in design costs far less than fixing them after release.
- 🚚 **Smoother supply chains.** Suppliers and buyers who reference the same standards misunderstand each other less.
- 🧾 **Audit readiness.** Documented procedures tied to requirements make audits calmer and faster.
- 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 **Consistent onboarding.** New hires learn one clear way of working instead of many informal ones.
- 🏆 **Reputation.** Demonstrated commitment to recognized standards strengthens customer confidence.
- 🔄 **Continuous improvement.** A written baseline makes it possible to measure progress and improve it.

### 🤖 For AI and data practitioners

- 🧪 **Domain grounding.** Engineering standards give precise terminology and structure for building and testing domain tools.
- 📏 **Evaluation.** Well-defined requirements make it easier to write objective test questions for AI assistants.
- 🗃️ **Better knowledge organization.** Glossaries, taxonomies, and knowledge graphs built from your own notes help tools find the right information.
- ⚖️ **Responsible use.** Working with licensed sources and original summaries keeps your projects on solid legal ground.

### 🌍 For communities and society

- 🏠 **Safer homes and workplaces.** Consistent requirements help protect everyday users of electrical and electronic products.
- 🌱 **Sustainability.** Shared efficiency and performance requirements support responsible use of energy and materials.
- 🔗 **Global cooperation.** International standards let countries and companies build technology that works together.
- 🎒 **Knowledge sharing.** Open, original explanations lower the barrier for people who are new to the field.

## ✍️ How to write good summaries

1. 🎯 **Start with the purpose.** Why does this standard exist, and who is it for?
2. 🧠 **Use your own words.** Rewrite ideas completely rather than copying sentences.
3. 🔢 **Keep it short.** A summary should be far shorter than the source.
4. 🔗 **Link to the source.** Always point to the official page so readers can get the full text.
5. 📌 **Cite the edition.** Requirements change, so record the year or edition you studied.
6. ⚠️ **Flag uncertainty.** If you are unsure, say so and tell readers to check the official document.
7. 🔄 **Review regularly.** Update summaries when a new edition is released.

## 📖 Beginner glossary

Short, general definitions in plain language. For precise terminology, see [Electropedia](https://www.electropedia.org).

| Term                      | Plain-language meaning                                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Standard**              | An agreed document describing requirements, methods, or guidelines so different people can work consistently |
| **Edition**               | A published version of a standard; newer editions can replace older ones                                     |
| **Amendment**             | An official change added to an existing standard                                                             |
| **Scope**                 | What a standard covers, and what it does not                                                                 |
| **Normative**             | Content that contains requirements you must follow to claim compliance                                       |
| **Informative**           | Content that gives guidance or examples but is not a requirement                                             |
| **Compliance**            | Meeting the requirements of a standard                                                                       |
| **Conformity assessment** | Checking, through testing or inspection, whether something meets requirements                                |
| **Gap analysis**          | Comparing what you do now with what a standard requires                                                      |
